Thuna Paha

තුන පහ
Blend

The blend this channel is named after — 'three-five', the classic Sinhala curry powder of coriander, cumin, fennel, cinnamon and curry leaves. Every Sri Lankan kitchen roasts its own, and no two houses taste the same.

Listen · 1:11

Roasted Curry Powder

බැදපු තුන පහ
Blend

Thuna paha's darker sibling: the same spices dry-roasted until deep brown. It powers black curries — jaffna crab, black pork curry — with a smoky depth raw powder can't reach.

Listen · 1:12

Ceylon Cinnamon

කුරුඳු
Bark & Root

Sri Lanka's gift to the world — true cinnamon, softer and sweeter than cassia, peeled by hand into feather-thin quills. The island still grows most of the world's supply, just like it did when spice ships fought over it.

Listen · 1:10

Turmeric

කහ
Bark & Root

The golden root that colours almost every Sri Lankan curry. A pinch goes into the pot first with onions and curry leaves — for colour, earthiness, and its famous anti-inflammatory punch.

Listen · 0:56

Ginger

ඉඟුරු
Bark & Root

Crushed with garlic into the base of most meat curries, brewed into plain tea for colds, and candied for sweets. Island ginger is small, fibrous and twice as sharp as the supermarket kind.

Listen · 1:04

Cardamom

එනසාල්
Fruit & Pod

The 'queen of spices' — green pods crushed into rice, milk toffee and curries alike. Sri Lankan cardamom grows in the misty hills of Kandy and gives watalappan its floral note.

Listen · 0:58

Cloves

කරාබු නැටි
Fruit & Pod

Dried flower buds that look like tiny nails and taste like warm perfume. Two or three go into a pot of yellow rice; chewing one is the island's oldest breath freshener.

Listen · 0:58

Nutmeg

සාදික්කා
Fruit & Pod

One tree, two spices: the seed is nutmeg, its red lace covering is mace. Grated fresh over milk rice and love cake — a little makes dessert sing, a lot makes you sleepy.

Listen · 1:17

Mace

වසාවාසි
Fruit & Pod

The crimson web wrapped around the nutmeg seed, milder and more floral than its twin. Prized in rich meat curries and the secret warmth in many Ceylon spice blends.

Listen · 1:01

Black Pepper

ගම්මිරිස්
Seed

Before chili ever reached the island, this was the heat. Sri Lankan black pepper is oily, bold and high in piperine — 'gammiris' still means serious spice in any village kitchen.

Listen · 1:00

Dried Chili

මිරිස්
Fruit & Pod

The Portuguese brought it; Sri Lanka never looked back. Sun-dried, flaked or ground — it's the fire in pol sambol and the red in every 'devilled' dish on the island.

Listen · 1:02

Coriander Seed

කොත්තමල්ලි
Seed

The biggest share of any curry powder — citrusy, nutty, gentle. Boiled whole it becomes kottamalli tea, the island's first medicine for every cold and fever.

Listen · 1:04

Cumin

සූදුරු
Seed

Earthy seeds that anchor thuna paha and rice pilafs. Roasted and ground with coriander and fennel, it's the backbone note your tongue reads as 'curry'.

Listen · 1:01

Fennel Seed

මාදුරු
Seed

Sweet, liquorice-scented seeds that round off the curry-powder trio. Chewed after meals as a digestive, and the quiet sweetness inside Sri Lankan fish curries.

Listen · 0:56

Fenugreek

උළුහාල්
Seed

Tiny bitter cubes that turn magical in coconut milk — they thicken and mellow white curries like kiri hodi. Soaked overnight, they're also a village remedy for the stomach.

Listen · 0:58

Mustard Seed

අබ
Seed

Popped in hot oil until they crackle — the opening beat of countless island dishes. Ground into a paste they sharpen pickles like malay achcharu.

Listen · 0:59

Curry Leaves

කරපිංචා
Leaf & Herb

Not 'curry flavour' — a tree of its own, and the single most Sri Lankan smell there is. A sprig sizzling in coconut oil with onions is how a thousand curries begin.

Listen · 1:13

Pandan (Rampe)

රම්පෙ
Leaf & Herb

The long green leaf knotted into every pot of rice and curry, lending a nutty, jasmine-like aroma. Sri Lankans call rice cooked without it 'naked'.

Listen · 1:03

Lemongrass (Sera)

සේර
Leaf & Herb

A bruised stalk in the curry pot brings citrus without the fruit. Paired with rampe and curry leaves, it forms the island's aromatic holy trinity.

Listen · 1:16

Garlic

සුදු ලූනු
Bark & Root

Whole cloves melt into dhal curry; crushed, it partners ginger at the base of nearly everything savoury. Garlic curry itself — sudu lunu curry — is a beloved dish, not just a seasoning.

Listen · 0:56

Tamarind

සියඹලා
Fruit & Pod

The sour soul of southern cooking. A lime-sized ball soaked in water gives fish ambul thiyal its dark tang and keeps it good for days without a fridge.

Listen · 1:16

Goraka

ගොරකා
Fruit & Pod

A smoked, dried garcinia fruit — blacker and smokier than tamarind. It's what makes true ambul thiyal 'sour fish curry' and not just fish curry.

Listen · 1:04

White Curry Powder

සුදු කරි කුඩු
Blend

The gentle blend for vegetable and coconut-milk curries — raw-ground coriander, cumin and fennel. White curries are the island's comfort food: mild, milky, everyday.

Listen · 1:06

Caraway (Sweet Cumin)

සූදුරු මාදුරු
Seed

Often confused with fennel in island markets, this warmer seed slips into biryani masalas and festive rice, a legacy of Malay and Moor kitchens on the coast.

Listen · 1:07
